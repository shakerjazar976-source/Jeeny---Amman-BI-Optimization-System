# Chapter 01: Introduction

## Background: BI as a managerial response to operational complexity

The digital transportation platforms provide for rich operational data. Every trip data includes data regarding the location, time, fare paid, trip duration, distance travelled, means of payment, risk category, and validity of services. The significance of such data lies in their ability to indicate the locations with the highest concentration of customers, areas where there is the shortage of drivers, routes requiring more time to travel than earning money, and trip types needing risk control measures. In this respect, BI is a natural management response to operational data complexity.

BI research papers always refer to dashboards as elements allowing transforming the raw data into a knowledge base. According to Goncalves et al. (2023), the BI system provides for data integration and transformation, KPI implementation, and visual analyses comprehensible to decision makers. These considerations are especially relevant to the Jeeny project because of the need to clean, validate, filter, and aggregate the data. Otherwise, the data will be presented in the form of figures, yet they will fail to deliver actionable insights.

Power BI is particularly suitable to be used in the process of descriptive and diagnostic analysis since it offers the possibility to make decision-making interactive with the use of filters, segments, and visualization elements. This solution was presented in another operational domain by Picozzi et al. (2024), who found several KPIs to build an “interactive dashboard, implemented using Power BI.” In the Jeeny project, this concept is used to monitor trips and revenue per city/zone, as well as operational load based on hour, pick-up, drop-off, risk, and efficiency of the route.

From the business perspective, there is one very simple but crucial step that needs to be done from the beginning of the project – determining which city to prioritize. As Amman holds the largest portion of trips and revenue, equal distribution of the analysis between all cities will significantly reduce its business value. Thus, the BI dashboard can serve as such a tool, identifying the operational priority, after which it opens a detailed view of inner zones, busiest hours, flights, and dangerous traffic areas.

## Ride-hailing as a spatial and temporal business problem

Unlike static enterprises, ride-hailing services are dynamic in nature as the demands keep changing spatially and temporally. A route that may be efficient in one hour might turn inefficient in another hour due to reasons like traffic congestion, availability of drivers, arrival of passengers from the airport. A seemingly average location in daily demand may become a bottleneck in an evening hour. Therefore, there are needs beyond counting in the analysis of ride-hailing services. City dominance, zone concentration, OD flow, demand in hours, revenue efficiency, and risk segmentation are required for this purpose.

As Jin et al. (2020) state, the city or the urban area is comprised of functional zones like residential, commercial, industrial, passenger transport, and tourist zones. The zones of Jeeny data set like Airport, Downtown, Business District, Mall Area, Residential, Port Area, Tourist Area, University Area, and Industrial Zone may be considered as functions. A journey from Airport to Downtown cannot be equated to a journey from Residential to Mall Area even if the latter is counted as a legitimate journey like the former. There may be differences in the demand, risk, and revenue efficiency of both journeys.

Moreover, there is evidence from the literature that demand forecasting and driver allocation cannot be considered in isolation. According to Rahman & Rifaat (2021), ride-hailing firms have to forecast not only their demand, but also the demand-supply balance to decrease passengers' wait time and the friction associated with driver searching. That is very important for this project. Power BI shows what happened, while Python helps us predict what is going to happen and how drivers should be distributed based on hourly and zone distribution.

For example, the Amman airport can serve as a perfect illustration of a bottleneck effect in space and time. Airport demand may increase dramatically at some hours, routes coming from the airport may be longer than others, and trips are riskier if they were made late at night or if the payment method was a cash-based one. The one-page city dashboard may indicate that Airport is among the leading pick-up zones; however, BI enhancement project must do much more than that.

## From descriptive BI to predictive and prescriptive analytics

A good BI project typically progresses through several steps. The first step involves the descriptive level, which means the questions, such as how many trips were made, where, and when? The second step involves the diagnostic level, where users find the answer to why there is pressure in one particular city, zone, or route. At the third step, users seek the predictive level, meaning where and when the pressure may repeat. At the final step, people find out how they should act. Thus, the Jeeny project starts with Power BI at two first levels and moves to Python at three others.

The staged approach can be justified from the perspective of existing research regarding dashboard systems and performance monitoring. According to Matheus et al. (2020), dashboard systems help users understand what happened and take action but also carry the risk of using inaccurate information or wrong conclusions and imposing predefined ideas on users. This aspect is especially important for the Jeeny project since an appealing visual interface does not guarantee anything. It is crucial to clean data, explain logic, and relate the indicators to business needs.

Another equally important layer is the predictive one. Saadi et al. (2017) discuss machine learning methods of short-term ride-hailing demand forecasting and make comparisons between decision trees, random forests, boosted decision trees, and neural networks using DiDi Chuxing data. The authors prove that ride-hailing demand forecasting is an applicable modeling task rather than a theoretical abstraction. For a student BI project, the model would not require the same complexity as ride-hailing applications; however, the concept should be proven that historical data about zone and hour can be applied for demand prediction and optimization of driver allocation.

Finally, the prescriptive layer can be presented via a Bottleneck Index and What-if analysis. Rather than pointing out the number of trips made by the company in the airport area, one would calculate a score based on the number of trips, share of revenue, average trip time, average distance, risk level, and peak-hour load factor. This results in creating a rank order of zones and origins-and-destinations. Further, decision-makers can analyze different scenarios including adding more drivers close to the airport during 17:00-21:00 hours, reviewing of underpriced trips, and controlling cash trips.

## Project problem statement

The main issue tackled by this project pertains to the lack of balance in operations within the ride-hailing network. As shown by the results of the dashboard, there is an inequality in demand, revenues, and pressure in terms of locations in Jordan or within Amman. There are cities where more trips occur, pickup areas that have more pressure, peak times, low revenue per minute OD pairs, and risky segments. These factors must be taken into account if the company wants to be able to efficiently allocate drivers.

From the perspective of BI, the following can describe the problem at hand: What are the steps necessary to transform the data on trip level ride-hailing information into decision support for identifying bottlenecks in Jordan/Amman, assessing route efficiency, identifying high-risk segments, predicting peak demand, and suggesting actions for driver allocations?

This issue is also associated with origin-destination demand. According to Wang et al. (2024), short-term ride-hailing OD demand prediction can be regarded as predicting the future OD matrices based on past observations as well as spatial-temporal relationships. Although the Jeeny project might involve less sophisticated analysis compared to cutting-edge machine learning techniques, the general principle behind that task is similar: a journey from point A to point B is a basic unit of analysis, and the system needs to comprehend inter-zonal travel rather than just pickups.

Thus, the project offers a solution oriented towards BI by incorporating Power BI visualizations, Python analysis, and management advice. While producing visuals is important for the project, it will deliver something beyond graphs since its expected outcome will include a complete rationale for prioritizing Amman, recognizing the airport as a special hub, evaluating routes for their price incentives, repositioning drivers, and risk-based operational control.

## Research objectives and contribution

The first objective is to create an effective descriptive perspective on trips, according to governorate, pick-up zone, destination zone, time of day, mode of payment, and risk class. The objective corresponds to the BI function of synthesizing information and making data understandable. It also helps management by reducing a trip table to relevant indicators, such as valid trips, revenue, average fare, average distance, average duration, high risk percent, and route efficiency.

The second objective is finding bottlenecks. For the purposes of this study, the definition of a bottleneck cannot be limited by trip volume alone. Instead, a real bottleneck should encompass high volume, high duration, great distance, poor revenue/min, and high risk, all at once. Accordingly, Amman Bottleneck Index (ABI) should be developed for ranking OD pairs and zones as low-pressure, mid-pressure, and high-pressure locations.

The third goal of this study is creating an improvement module utilizing Python coding language. Such a module will be able to check data for its quality, compute new KPIs, rank OD pairs, estimate hourly demand, provide driver distribution advice, and output scenarios. It is vital since the BI project in question is not only supposed to explain what happened but also advise on actions that need to be taken next. The Python layer brings such a perspective into this study.

The last objective of this project is formulating recommendations based on existing literature. The project relies on the following academic studies: BI dashboard research, KPI monitoring techniques, ride-hailing demand prediction methods, OD analysis approaches, issues connected to the supply-and-demand mismatch, driver repositioning strategies, and built environment research. What is unique about this research is the way these concepts are combined to create the basis for the improvement of Jordan's situation, specifically, Amman's.
